Heating and Air Conditioning Service Across San Francisco

Heating and cooling around here means a wide mix of equipment: older gas furnaces in established neighborhoods, newer heat pumps in recent construction, ductless mini-splits in additions and converted garages, and rooftop units on commercial buildings. Our team handles all of it. We have years of field experience, and we've learned how the local climate, the housing stock, and code requirements affect the way a system runs and fails.

Most homeowners we meet need one of three things: a repair, a replacement, or a seasonal tune-up. Repairs tend to be quick: a burned contactor, a corroded flame sensor, a slow refrigerant leak, or a worn-out blower motor. Replacements get a proper load calculation, not a guess based on the old unit's tonnage, which keeps temperatures even and stops the new equipment from short-cycling itself to an early death. Tune-ups cover coil cleaning, a refrigerant charge check, safety control testing, and a static pressure reading, which does more than anything else to extend the life of your system.

Local businesses get the same attention. A hot dining room or a drafty office costs real money, so our commercial crews focus on getting to the cause quickly and keep common parts on board for rooftop units, split systems, and ventilation equipment. Property managers, restaurant owners, and retailers can count on us to schedule work around their operating hours whenever we can.

Fast HVAC Response in San Francisco

When your heat or air quits, how quickly help arrives matters more than anything else. For that reason, we keep our schedule flexible across the area and put a real person on the phone. Urgent calls typically see a technician within a day, and we treat a failed furnace in a cold snap or a dead AC in the heat as top priority.

The emergency calls we run usually follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's an AC blowing warm air, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil frozen solid. On the heating side, it's a furnace that lights and then shuts down. Year-round, it's breakers that trip every cycle, clogged condensate drains overflowing into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that stop responding. Because our trucks are stocked with capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, most of these get fixed in one visit, with the price agreed before any work begins.

Two situations call for quick action from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so warm indoor air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that keeps cycling on and off or produces a burning smell. Shut it down at the switch and call us. That small step can prevent a far bigger repair.

How San Francisco Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Routine maintenance is the difference between a system that runs for years and one that breaks down in the middle of a heat wave. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: fresh filters every one to three months, at least two feet of open space around the outdoor condenser, a spring rinse of the condenser coil, and two professional tune-ups a year, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners last 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and starting to struggle, such as rising energy bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or repairs stacking up season after season, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one in the middle of a cold snap.

Minor issues don't stay minor when they're ignored. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but left alone for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and just like that a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a miserable night.
